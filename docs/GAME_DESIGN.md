# Game Design Foundation

**Status:** High-level direction approved; detailed rules remain to be designed.

## 1. World

Popoly takes place in an alternative near-future around 2035. The setting should feel plausible and technologically recognizable without directly reproducing real companies, countries, or current political institutions.

The world contains multiple regions with distinct economic conditions, resources, infrastructure, institutions, risks, and development paths. The exact number and boundaries of regions are open design questions.

## 2. The player

The player begins as the leader or controlling force behind a corporate group / holding structure.

The player may eventually found and develop companies; acquire ownership and control; create subsidiaries and holding structures; raise capital, borrow, issue equity or debt; invest in research and infrastructure; negotiate contracts and strategic partnerships; conduct intelligence and counterintelligence operations; build relationships with institutions and governments; and influence regulation, public policy, and regional development.

The player should not automatically own or directly command every organization they influence. Ownership, control, contractual power, reputation, and political influence are distinct forms of leverage.

## 3. Independent actors

The world must contain actors with goals and agency of their own.

### Corporations
Companies operate, compete, invest, innovate, hire leadership, form alliances, take risks, and can fail. Their behavior should be shaped by their capabilities, finances, strategy, leadership, and external conditions.

### States and institutions
Governments and institutions pursue public, political, economic, and security objectives. They can regulate, tax, subsidize, procure, investigate, sanction, negotiate, and compete for strategic advantages.

### Other actors
The design may later include investors, research institutions, media groups, influential individuals, civil organizations, and criminal or covert networks. Add them only when they create useful systemic interactions.

## 4. Gradual expansion of player influence

The chosen hybrid direction has a deliberate progression:

1. **Corporate foundation:** build or acquire businesses, manage capital, compete, and establish credibility.
2. **Market influence:** affect sectors, supply chains, standards, and strategic dependencies.
3. **Institutional access:** gain relationships, contracts, lobbying capacity, and political relevance.
4. **Regional influence:** shape infrastructure, investment, technology adoption, and policy outcomes in particular regions.
5. **World-shaping power:** influence international alignments, global standards, critical systems, and the direction of the wider world.

This is a conceptual progression, not a fixed linear technology tree. Different strategies may unlock different forms of influence, and rivals or states may resist.

## 5. Strategic dimensions

The game should support several complementary paths to power:

- **Financial:** capital, ownership, financing, investment reach.
- **Technological:** research, patents, expertise, standards, innovation ecosystems.
- **Industrial and infrastructural:** production capacity, energy, compute, logistics, communications, critical materials.
- **Political and institutional:** access, legitimacy, policy influence, contracts, regulatory outcomes.
- **Intelligence:** information advantage, covert networks, counterintelligence, strategic exposure.
- **Reputational and social:** trust, credibility, public support, talent attraction, stakeholder relationships.

No path should be a guaranteed shortcut. Each creates dependencies, costs, and vulnerabilities.

## 6. Core gameplay cycle

The intended strategic cycle is:

1. **Observe:** review the state of the world, reports, markets, actors, risks, and opportunities.
2. **Interpret:** decide what matters, what is uncertain, and what may happen next.
3. **Plan:** allocate capital, time, organizational capacity, and political or informational resources.
4. **Act:** make investments, deals, research choices, organizational changes, or strategic operations.
5. **Resolve:** the world and independent actors react; systems update and consequences propagate.
6. **Adapt:** review outcomes and revise the strategy.

The exact time model (turn-based, continuous with pauses, or hybrid) is intentionally undecided.

## 7. Victory and long-term objectives

Victory must not reduce to net worth alone. Potential forms of dominance include financial, technological, infrastructural, political, and intelligence-based influence.

The final scoring or victory model remains open. It should reward coherent strategic achievement while allowing different playstyles and meaningful recovery from setbacks.

## 8. Presentation and player understanding

The interface should feel like a strategic command environment, not a generic financial dashboard.

Potential top-level views: Command Center; World Map; Corporation; Markets; Technology; Intelligence; Politics; News and Events.

These are navigation concepts, not a final screen specification. The main view should quickly answer: What is happening in the world? What is happening to my organization? Who or what threatens my position? What opportunities have appeared? Which decisions deserve attention now?

## 9. Open design questions

Do not silently treat these as settled: exact map structure and regional scale; number and types of starting actors; time progression and action economy; depth of corporate governance and ownership; how influence is measured and contested; how governments and institutions are represented; victory, campaign length, and sandbox options; the balance between authored events and emergent simulation.
