# Popoly: Tech Dominion — Vision

**Status:** Foundation approved; details are subject to design iteration  
**Setting:** Alternative near-future, approximately 2035  
**Genre direction:** Hardcore grand strategy / economic simulation / evolving sandbox

## One-sentence vision

Popoly is a deep strategy game in which the player rises from a corporate power into a force capable of reshaping economies, technology, institutions, and eventually the political direction of the world.

## The player fantasy

The player is not simply trying to accumulate the largest bank balance. They are building and directing an organization, identifying leverage points in a complex world, competing with independent powers, and gradually gaining the ability to influence how that world works.

Power can come from capital and ownership; productive companies and supply chains; technology, patents, and research capacity; infrastructure and scarce resources; information, intelligence, and reputation; and political access and institutional influence.

Wealth is an instrument and a measure of capacity, not the only purpose of play.

## Chosen direction: the hybrid model (C)

The game begins with a strong corporate-strategy identity. The player initially acts through a corporate group or holding structure. As the organization grows, it can gain influence over regions, institutions, markets, and public policy.

The long-term ambition is an expanding sandbox. Corporations remain important, but they become one of several instruments through which the player can shape the world. This expansion must be earned through gameplay and consequences; it is not unrestricted control from the opening turn.

## Experience pillars

1. **Strategic agency.** Important decisions should have meaningful trade-offs and more than one viable approach.
2. **An independent world.** Companies, states, competitors, and other actors pursue their own interests whether or not the player intervenes.
3. **Interconnected systems.** Economic, technological, political, and intelligence systems should influence one another.
4. **Emergent history.** Events should arise from the state of the simulation as well as from authored scenarios.
5. **Information as power.** The player should make decisions with incomplete, delayed, uncertain, or contested information.
6. **Consequences over button pressing.** Actions should change incentives, relationships, capabilities, and future opportunities.
7. **Hardcore depth, readable presentation.** Complexity belongs in the simulation; the interface must make it understandable.

## Design principles

- No feature exists only to fill a menu or checklist.
- Avoid a single dominant strategy or one-dimensional victory condition.
- Failure should create new problems and possibilities, not automatically end an interesting story.
- Automation may reduce repetitive administration, but must preserve meaningful strategic decisions.
- Scale should emerge gradually. Do not simulate enormous detail before the core loop is enjoyable.
- Prefer coherent, explainable rules over arbitrary bonuses and hidden exceptions.

## Initial scope boundaries

The first playable foundation should focus on a small number of actors and a limited set of tightly connected systems.

Explicitly deferred until the core is proven: full-scale military warfare and detailed armed forces; hundreds of individually simulated goods and production recipes; simulation of every employee as an individual; thousands of active companies; full multiplayer infrastructure; and blockchain mechanics.

These are scope boundaries for early development, not permanent prohibitions.

## Success criteria for the project

Popoly is moving in the right direction when a player can understand the current strategic situation; identify a meaningful opportunity or threat; choose between competing uses of limited resources; see the world and rival actors respond; understand why important outcomes occurred; and form a long-term strategy that can adapt when circumstances change.

The first test is not the number of features. It is whether a compact simulation already produces compelling decisions and stories.
